# M107: A model specification with a formula or recipe as the orchestrators' input

**Status:** done (2026-09-21, PR #122 https://github.com/tidymodels/nestedtune/pull/122)

**Goal:** A user who calls `tune_grid(spec, preprocessor, resamples)` can call `nested_tune_grid(spec, preprocessor, resamples)` and its five siblings the same way.

**Outcome:** `nested_tune_grid()`, `nested_tune_bayes()`, `nested_tune_race_anova()`, `nested_tune_race_win_loss()`, `nested_tune_sim_anneal()` and `nested_fit_resamples()` are S3 generics on `object`. The `workflow` method keeps the old signature. The `model_spec` method takes `(object, preprocessor, resamples, ...)` and accepts a formula or a recipe. It calls the generic again on `workflows::workflow(preprocessor, object)` inside `with_user_call()`. That helper gives errors from the inner call the user's call. The `default` method refuses any other object. A missing or wrong preprocessor raises `nestedtune_bad_preprocessor`. A preprocessor beside a workflow raises `nestedtune_preprocessor_with_workflow`, whether by name, in `resamples`, or unnamed in the dots. A missing `resamples` or `object` is refused by `rlang::check_required()`. The racer and annealer spec methods check finetune first. `orchestrator_args()` reads each `workflow` method's formals. `check_workflow()` names the `workflow(preprocessor, spec)` wrapping for `nested_final_fit()`. The results are identical to the workflow route under one seed, for a formula and a recipe, and so is the final fit's `predict()` output.

**Decisions:** D-069 (the generic signature in tune's order), D-070 (corrects D-069's reason for refusing `workflow_variables()` and its list of inputs refused beside a workflow).
