# M126: Edge cases of averaged predictions

**Status:** done (2026-09-28, PR #141 https://github.com/tidymodels/nestedtune/pull/141)

**Goal:** `collect_predictions(summarize = TRUE)` and `augment()` refuse by name three saved-prediction shapes their shared average misreads, and on a benchmark table the average runs in at most a quarter of its M124 time.

**Outcome:** `collect_predictions(summarize = TRUE)` runs `check_predictions_rows()`, with class `nestedtune_collect_predictions_predictions`. Its message points to `summarize = FALSE`, and the per-fold table is not checked. `check_average_columns()` refuses three shapes with `nestedtune_summarize_columns`. They are two factor outcome columns, a `.pred_class` with no factor outcome, and a censored `.pred` entry that is not NULL and has no `.eval_time`. `factor_outcomes()` reads the outcomes. `average_survival()` leaves a NULL entry out and gives NULL to a row with no entries. `augment()` checks a name collision before the quantile and shape refusals. The average uses `mean_by()`, grouped `rowsum()` sums with `mean()`'s second pass, and `median_by()`, one sort. `benchmarks/averaging-speed.R` compares it with a frozen M124 copy on three tables of 33,334 rows, each held out by 3 distinct folds. The last run gave a ratio of 0.090, and all three tables agreed. Both help pages say a metric on averaged predictions is not the nested estimate and name `collect_metrics()`. `DESIGN.md` Known issues lists the two shapes still averaged without a refusal.

**Decisions:** D-094 extends D-090 and D-092's order of refusals.

**Review:** Two passes. Pass 1 returned AC6, a defect return, because the benchmark dealt folds so that one fold held some rows more than once. Pass 2 passed all eight criteria. Its diff-bug reader found a tie defect. Without `mean()`'s second pass, a tie between `c(0.6, 0.4, 0.2)` and `c(0.2, 0.4, 0.6)` went to the later level. At the gate, findings 1, 2, 5, 9, 10 and 11 were fixed. The fixes were the second pass, a tie test that fails without it, and empty groups in `mean_by()`. They also covered the row-check message and the NEWS and help wording. Findings 3, 4 and 8 went to one candidate row, and 6, 7 and 12 were rejected. CI on x86_64 Linux then failed the new test's premise, because `mean()` sums in `long double` there. The premise now reads in `double` arithmetic, and 14 checks passed. `devtools::check()` gave 0 errors, 0 warnings and 0 notes. Nothing was graduated or retired.
